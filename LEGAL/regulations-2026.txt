Foreword

The Independent National Electoral Commission (herein referred to as the Commission),
pursuant to Section 160(1) of the Constitution of the Federal Republic of Nigeria, 1999 (as
amended) and Section 151 of the Electoral Act, 2026, hereby issues the Regulations and
Guidelines for the Conduct of Elections, 2026. It replaces the Regulations and Guidelines for the
Conduct of Elections, 2022, which was made under the repealed Electoral Act, 2022.

The Commission is empowered under Paragraph 15, Third Schedule, Part |, of the Constitution to
conduct elections into various political offices created by the Constitution.

These Regulations and Guidelines reflect the Commission’s commitment to transparent,
credible, technology-driven and legally compliant electoral processes. They provide the
operational framework for the conduct of elections and give practical effect to the provisions of the
Electoral Act, 2026, while responding to lessons from previous elections and evolving
developments in election administration.

In developing these Regulations and Guidelines, the Commission undertook extensive
consultations and considered judicial decisions, reports of election observers, and submissions
from political parties, security agencies, civil society organisations, development partners and
other stakeholders. The result is a more comprehensive and responsive regulatory framework
aimed at deepening public confidence in the electoral process.

A number of important innovations have been introduced to strengthen electoral integrity,
transparency and accountability.

Notably, the Regulations provide a clearer framework regarding ballot papers bearing the official
mark prescribed by the Commission. The provisions define the components of the official mark,
including the official stamp, signature, date and serial number traceable to the ballot booklet
issued for the election. They also establish procedures for the treatment, documentation,
reporting and escalation of ballot papers rejected for lack of official marks, while preserving
statutory safeguards relating to the consideration of such ballots in circumstances of a tie as
permitted under the Electoral Act, 2026. These measures are intended to reinforce ballot
authenticity, prevent manipulation and strengthen the integrity of the vote-counting process.

The Regulations also introduce clearer procedures for the electronic transmission and verification
of polling unit results through the Bimodal Voter Accreditation System (BVAS) and the INEC
Result Viewing Portal (IReV). They provide for the electronic transmission of validated polling unit
results and scanned copies of Forms EC8Aseries directly from the Polling Unit, while establishing
transparent contingency procedures in the event of transmission failure. The framework ensures
that election officials, polling agents and stakeholders are informed of technical challenges,
provides for institutional verification of transmission failures at the collation level, and preserves
the integrity of the process through the use of duly authenticated result forms in accordance with
the Electoral Act, 2026, and these Regulations and Guidelines. These provisions are designed to
enhance transparency, result traceability and public confidence in the management of election
results.

Furthermore, these Regulations and Guidelines strengthen procedures for result management
and collation, improve accountability mechanisms for election officials, enhance the deployment
and management of BVAS, and simplify aspects of the collation framework through the
harmonisation of relevant forms and procedures.

To address the logistical challenges posed by large polling units, the procedure to be followed
where multiple Bimodal Voter Accreditation System (BVAS) devices are deployed is also

Regulations and Guidelines for the Conduct
of Elections, 2026

In exercise of the powers conferred by the Constitution of the Federal Republic of
Nigeria 1999 (as amended) and the Electoral Act 2026, the Independent National
Electoral Commission (INEC) herein referred to as “the Commission” issues the
following regulations and guidelines for the conduct of Elections (General Election,
Off-Cycle Elections, Bye-elections, Re-run Elections, Supplementary Election and
Federal Capital Territory (FCT) - Area Councils Election). These Regulations and
Guidelines are issued pursuant to section 160 (1) of the Constitution of the Federal
Republic of Nigeria 1999 (as amended) and Section 151 of the Electoral Act, 2026.
They supersede all other regulations and/or guidelines on the conduct of elections
issued by the Commission and shall remain in force until replaced by new Regulations
and Guidelines or updated by way of revisions or supplementary regulations and
guidelines supported by Decision Extracts of the Commission or an official gazette.

PART I—ELECTIONS AND ARRANGEMENTS FOR THEIR CONDUCT

1. These Regulations and Guidelines shall apply to the conduct of elections to the
following offices:

(i) President and Vice President;

(ii) Governor and Deputy Governor;

(iii) National Assembly (Senate and House of Representatives);
(iv) State Houses of Assembly;

(v) Chairmen and Vice-Chairmen of the Federal Capital Territory (FCT)
Area Councils; and

(vi) Councillors of Federal Capital Territory (FCT) Area Councils
Legislature.

2. Election to the office of President and Vice President as well as National
Assembly shall hold on the Third Saturday of January of any General Election
year or as may be determined by the Commission, while election to the Office
of Governor and Deputy Governor and the State Houses of Assembly shall
hold three (3) weeks thereafter.

3. An off-cycle Governorship election shall hold on the Saturday following the
100th day to the end of the tenure of the incumbent, or as may be determined
by the Commission.

4. (a) Whenever the end of tenure of Federal Capital Territory (FCT) Area
Councils coincides with a General Election year, election into the office of
Chairman and Vice Chairman and Councillors of the Area Councils shall be
held with the election for Governor and Deputy Governor and the State Houses
of Assembly.

(b) Where the end of tenure of Federal Capital Territory (FCT) Area
Councils does not coincide with a General Election year, the election to
the office of Chairman and Vice-Chairman and Councillors of the Area
Councils shall hold on the Saturday following the 100th day to the end
of the tenure of the incumbent, or as may be determined by the
Commission.

Aperson shall be eligible to vote at an election conducted by the Commission if:

(i) He/she is a Nigerian Citizen
(ii) He/she is a registered voter
(iii) His/her name appears in the Register of Voters and

(iv) He/she presents a valid Permanent Voter's Card (PVC)/ a
downloadable copy (Section 18(1) of the Electoral Act 2026) at his/her
Polling Unit and

(v) His/her biometric data is authenticated by the Bimodal Voter
Accreditation System (BVAS).

Poll Officials, Collation and Returning Officers as appointees of the
Commission and Security Officials on election duty shall abide by the oath of
loyalty and neutrality for Election Officials, as provided in Section 26(1) of the
Electoral Act 2026, and act in accordance with extant Regulations and
Guidelines of the Commission.

Security Agents on election duty shall:

i. Provide security at the Polling Units/Polling Stations and Collation
Centres to ensure that the Polling Units, counting of ballots, collation
and declaration of results are conducted without any disturbance;

ii. Take necessary measures to prevent violence or any activity that can
threaten to disrupt the elections;

iii. Comply with any lawful directive(s) issued by or under the authority of

INEC;

iv. Ensure the safety and security of all election personnel and materials by
escorting and guarding the materials at all levels as appropriate;

Vv. Arrest on the instruction of the Presiding Officer or other INEC officials,

any person(s) causing any disturbance or preventing the smooth
conduct of proceedings at Polling Units/Stations and Collation Centres;

Vi. On the instruction of the Presiding Officer, stand at the end of the queue
of voters at the Polling Unit at the official close of poll to prevent any
person joining in;

Vii. Escort the Presiding Officer and other election officials to deliver the
election results, ballot boxes and other election materials safely to the
RA/Ward Collation Centre; and

viii. | Escort Collation Officers to deliver election results to the Returning
Officer and subsequently to the Resident Electoral Commissioner or
Electoral Officer, as the case may be, for the submission of election
materials and results.

ix. Work harmoniously with the election officials and not interfere/disrupt
the Electoral process.

Voting in any election to which these regulations and guidelines apply shall
take place at Polling Units established by the Commission.

(a) Whenever practicable, Polling Units shall be located within enclosures in
public places accessible to every voter, including Persons with Disability
(PWDs). Where they are in open spaces, canopies may be provided.

(b) Public places include public schools, civic centres, town halls and
communal open spaces. Polling Units shall not be in places of worship,
palaces of traditional rulers and private homes.

“Accreditation” means the process of verifying and authenticating the
identity and eligibility of a voter at a Polling Unit using the Bimodal Voter
Accreditation System (BVAS) or any other device approved by the
Commission before such voter is allowed to vote.
“Act” means the ElectoralAct, 2026.
“Assistant Presiding Officer (APO)” means an election official appointed by

voting, counting of votes and such other functions as may be assigned under
these Regulations and Guidelines.
“Bimodal Voter Accreditation System (BVAS)” means the electronic
device approved and deployed by the Commission for the accreditation and
authentication of voters through fingerprint and facial recognition technology
and for such other electoral functions as may be prescribed by the
Commission.
“Bye-Election” means an election conducted by the Commission to fill a
vacancy occurring in an elective office before the expiration of the tenure of the
holder of that office.
“Candidate” means a person validly nominated by a Political Party and
whose name has been submitted to and accepted by the Commission for an
election under the Constitution, the Act and these Regulations and
Guidelines.
“Certificate of Return” means the certificate issued by the Commission to a
candidate returned as elected in an election conducted under the
Constitution, the Act and these Regulations and Guidelines.
“Chairman” means the Chairman of the Commission appointed pursuant to
section 154 of the Constitution or any person appointed to act in that capacity
for the time being.
“Chief Electoral Commissioner” means the Chairman of the Commission
who serves as the Returning Officer for the Presidential Election pursuant to
the Constitution, the Act and these Regulations and Guidelines.
“Collation Officer” means a person appointed by the Commission to collate
election results at a designated Collation Centre.
